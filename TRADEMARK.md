# OneVault Trademark Policy

"OneVault", the OneVault logo (the keyhole mark) and the OneVault wordmark
are trademarks of the OneVault maintainer ([Amine-AD](https://github.com/Amine-AD)),
whether registered or not. The source code is open source under the
[AGPL-3.0](LICENSE). That license covers the code only; it does not give any
rights to the name or logo.

The goal is simple: anyone can truthfully say they use, build on or
contribute to OneVault, but nobody should suggest an official connection that
doesn't exist. For a password manager this matters more than usual: people
need to know whose server holds their vault.

## You can, without asking

- Use and self-host OneVault, changed or not, for yourself, your family or
  your organization, and say that you do.
- Say true things like "built on OneVault", "a fork of OneVault",
  "compatible with OneVault" or "we host OneVault for our clients".
- Use the name "OneVault" to refer to the project in articles, videos,
  tutorials, talks and comparisons.
- Link to the project and use unmodified screenshots of it.

## You need written permission to

- Use "OneVault", or a name that could be confused with it, in the name of
  your company, product, service, domain, social media account or app store
  listing (for example "OneVault Cloud" or "onevault-hosting.com").
- Offer a hosted, managed or modified version in a way that suggests it is
  the official OneVault service, or is endorsed by or affiliated with it.
- Use the OneVault logo as your own logo, or as part of your branding.
- Sell merchandise with the OneVault name or logo.

## Forks and hosted copies

You can maintain, distribute and host forks under the terms of the AGPL-3.0.
If you do so publicly, give your version its own name and logo, and replace
the OneVault branding in the app (the logo files in `public/assets/logos/`
and `src/components/BrandLogo.tsx`). You may say it is "a fork of OneVault"
or "based on OneVault".

## Questions

If you're not sure whether something is allowed, or want permission, open an
issue on GitHub. Asking costs nothing, and the answer is usually yes.
